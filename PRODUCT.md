# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- **Students** (mostly Kurdish- and Arabic-speaking English learners) taking the free placement test on the public site. Many arrive on a phone; reading passages are easier on a laptop or tablet, so phones get a friendly recommendation, never a block.
- **Admins and managers** (non-technical teachers and staff) maintaining courses, ESL resources and the placement quiz in the admin dashboard, usually on a laptop, in short sessions between classes.

## Product Purpose

Tutelage teaches languages. The site publishes courses and free ESL material and runs a timed placement test that tells a student their CEFR level (A1 to C2). The dashboard lets staff manage all of it without a developer. Success: a student finishes the test without confusion and trusts the level they get; a teacher sets up a section, a passage and its questions without asking for help.

## Brand Personality

Clean, learning-first, friendly academic. Calm and encouraging, never playful or loud. During the test it feels like a well-made exam booklet: quiet surfaces, readable type, the brand yellow only on the current step and the next action.

## Anti-references

- Gamified quiz apps (confetti, streaks, bouncing progress, emoji).
- Generic SaaS dashboard templates: icon-in-circle KPI tiles, gradients, coloured sidebars.
- Exam software that feels hostile: red countdowns, walls of instructions, cramped text.

## Design Principles

1. **Reading comes first.** Long text gets a real measure, generous leading and its own scroll; nothing competes with it.
2. **Say it in the user's words.** "Questions 1 to 4", "Write a new question", not ids, slugs or system terms.
3. **Show the structure.** When something has a place (a passage inside a section's questions), draw the place instead of describing it.
4. **Guide, don't block.** Recommend (use a bigger screen), warn (range overlaps), and let people continue where it's safe.
5. **One way to do each thing.** Same controls, same states, same words across the dashboard.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast, visible focus (brand ring), full keyboard use, labelled controls, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected. Students read in a second language, so copy stays short and plain. The site also runs in Kurdish (RTL); layouts use logical properties where direction matters.
